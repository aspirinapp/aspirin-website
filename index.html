Most medicine mistakes happen at home, unnoticed.

The same active ingredient under two brand names. Two medicines that don’t mix. Tablets taken at the wrong time. Many older people take several medicines from different doctors, and no one sees the full list.

Package leaflets are long and hard to read, and abroad they’re in a language you may not know. aspirin.app gives you a short summary checked by a pharmacist, with a link to the full original.

1. What this medicine is and what it is used for

This medicine contains the active substance ibuprofen, which belongs to a group of medicines called non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drugs (NSAIDs). It is used for the short-term relief of mild to moderate pain, such as headache, dental pain and period pain, and to reduce fever.

2. What you need to know before you take it

Do not take this medicine if you are allergic to ibuprofen or any of the other ingredients, if you have had a stomach ulcer or bleeding, or if you are in the last three months of pregnancy. Talk to your doctor or pharmacist before taking it if you have asthma, kidney, liver or heart problems, or high blood pressure.

Tell your pharmacist if you are taking medicines that thin the blood or low-dose acetylsalicylic acid. Taking this medicine with other NSAIDs may increase the risk of side effects.

3. How to take it

Adults and adolescents from 12 years: take the lowest effective dose for the shortest time needed. Take with or after food. Leave at least 6 hours between doses. Do not take more than stated in the dosage instructions in 24 hours.

If you forget to take a dose, do not take a double dose to make up for a forgotten one.

4. Possible side effects

Like all medicines, this medicine can cause side effects. Stop taking it and seek medical help immediately if you notice black, tarry stools or vomit blood, or signs of a severe allergic reaction such as swelling of the face or difficulty breathing.

5. How to store it

Keep out of the sight and reach of children. Do not use after the expiry date stated on the carton. Do not throw away medicines via wastewater or household waste; ask your pharmacist how to dispose of them.

    Safeguards

    Six rules built into our AI.

    No medical facts from memory

    The AI never makes up doses, ingredients or interactions. It only rewords verified sources.

    Nothing medical written on the fly

    Summaries are written once per medicine, checked and then fixed. Users only see approved text.

    Never “safe to take”

    The app explains what is known and points you to a professional. It doesn’t approve or prescribe anything.

    Asks when unsure

    If the app isn’t sure what it scanned, it asks you to compare with the box before saving.

    Traceable

    Every warning shows where its data comes from and which version was used.

    Emergency first

    Signs of an overdose or a serious reaction? The app always shows the emergency number 112 first.

    Regulation

    Built to medical device standards from day one.

    Under EU rules (the Medical Device Regulation, MDR), software that checks interactions for a specific person is generally a medical device of at least Class IIa. We launch step by step, so people benefit early while certification is under way.

    FAQ

    Questions we often hear.

    Does aspirin.app give medical advice?

    No. It organises your medicines, explains official information in plain words and warns you about known risks based on verified data. Decisions about treatment are made by you and your doctor or pharmacist.

    How do you stop the AI from making mistakes?

    The AI only recognises packaging and rewords approved text. Every medical statement comes from licensed drug data, summaries are checked by a pharmacist, and you confirm anything the app isn’t sure about.

    Is it a medical device?

    The medicine cabinet features are an information tool. Under EU rules, software that checks interactions is generally a medical device of at least Class IIa, so we’ll offer it through a certified module first while we prepare our own CE marking.

    Where is my data stored?

    In the European Union, encrypted. Photos of boxes are deleted once they’ve been read. You can download or delete all your data at any time.

    Which countries and languages?

    The app is designed for the whole EU. It launches in English, German, Spanish, French and Ukrainian, with more languages to follow.

    Will there be ads from drug companies?

    Never. We don’t accept product placement and don’t sell data. We earn money from subscriptions and from partners who benefit when medicines are taken safely.

    How does family mode protect my privacy?

    Each person decides who can see their list and get alerts. They can change their mind at any time.

    When can I start using it?

    We’re building the prototype now. The first users join through our founding programme in 2027. Sign up and we’ll be in touch.

    Is aspirin.app connected to the maker of Aspirin®?

    No. aspirin.app is an independent project. Aspirin® is a registered trademark of Bayer AG.
